Rule: if your headline could sit on any other landing page in any other niche, rewrite it. 2. The 1-Column Layout No sidebars. No navigation menu. No unrelated images. No links to your blog, your social profiles, or your "About" page. Here's why: every link is an exit. A landing page with a menu bar is a landing page giving people permission to leave before converting. The visitor should have exactly two options: Give you their email, or Close the tab That's it. No third option. No "browse around first." This feels aggressive to beginners — it isn't. It's just decision architecture. You're not trying to be a website. You're trying to be a single transaction. 3. The Call to Action (CTA) "Learn More" and "Submit" are conversion killers. They're vague, they carry no value proposition, and they ask the visitor to trust a button that tells them nothing. Replace vague verbs with specific outcomes: "Send me the template" "Get instant access" "Built with Systeme.io" A specific CTA restates the value at the exact moment of decision. The visitor already knows what they're getting because the button just told them again. That's not a small copywriting tweak — it's often the single highest-leverage change you can make on a page.
